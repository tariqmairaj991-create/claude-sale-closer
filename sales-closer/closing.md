# Closing

Closing is asking for the decision clearly after you've earned it. If discovery and the offer are strong, closing is a short, calm moment.

## 1. The Close Flow (full call)

1. **Frame the call**
2. **Discover** (see `discovery.md`)
3. **Reflect back** what you heard
4. **Present the offer**, matched to their words
5. **State the price**, then stop
6. **Ask for the decision**
7. **Handle concerns** (see `objections.md`)
8. **Lock the next step**

### 1. Frame the call (first 30 seconds)

> "Here's how I'd like to use our time: I'll ask some questions about what's going on, tell you honestly if I think I can help, and if so, show you how it works. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you that too. Sound good?"

Why: removes pressure, sets you as an advisor and gets agreement to the structure.

### 4. Present the offer

Structure:
1. Restate their goal and problem in one sentence.
2. Show the path: "Here's how we'd get you there" in 3-4 steps.
3. Tie each step to something they told you.
4. Mention proof (a similar client and result).
5. Mention risk reversal.

Keep it under 3-4 minutes. Skip anything they don't care about.

### 5. State the price

> "The investment is $X. [Pause]"

Don't explain, justify or discount. Let them respond.

### 6. Ask for the decision

Direct options:
- "Would you like to get started?"
- "Does this feel like the right fit for you?"
- "Shall we get you set up today?"

Assumptive (once they've signaled interest):
- "Great. Let's get you started. Would you prefer to pay by card or invoice?"

## 2. Types of closes

| Close | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Strong fit, clear interest | "Do you want to move forward?" |
| **Assumptive** | Buying signals present | "Which start date works, Monday or the 15th?" |
| **Summary** | After a long conversation | "You wanted A, B and C. This gives you all three. Shall we begin?" |
| **Option** | Two good choices | "Would you prefer the standard or premium package?" |
| **Trial** | Hesitant, risk-focused | "Let's start with a 2-week pilot and decide after." |
| **Conditional** | Objection is the only barrier | "If we solve [concern], are you ready to start?" |
| **Scale** | Unclear readiness | "On a scale of 1-10, how ready are you? What would make it higher?" |

## 3. Buying signals

- They ask about price, timing, onboarding or payment.
- They speak as if they already own it ("when we start...").
- They ask about specifics of results or support.
- They bring in another decision-maker.
- Their body language or tone relaxes.

When you see signals, stop selling and ask for the decision.

## 4. Silence

After the price or the ask, stop talking. Count to ten in your head. Whoever speaks first usually gives ground.

## 5. After a "yes"

- Confirm clearly: what happens next, when, and what you need from them.
- Collect payment or signed agreement on the call when possible.
- Send a welcome message within an hour.
- Reduce buyer's remorse: restate why they decided and what the first win will be.

## 6. After a "no"

- Thank them genuinely.
- Ask: "Could I ask what the deciding factor was?"
- Offer a future check-in date.
- Log the reason. Patterns show you what to fix.

## 7. After "I'll think about it"

Don't accept it silently, and don't pressure.

> "Of course. What specifically do you want to think over? ... Great. When shall we talk again, Thursday at 2?"

Book the next call before you hang up.

## 8. Follow-up cadence

- **Same day:** short recap and agreed next step.
- **Day 2-3:** helpful addition (case, answer to a question).
- **Day 7:** check on the decision, ask what's open.
- **Day 14:** final respectful message with a clear yes/no ask.
- After that, move to a long-term nurture list.

Always add value. "Just checking in" adds nothing.

## 9. Close tracker

Track for each call: showed up (y/n), qualified (y/n), offer presented (y/n), outcome, objection raised, next step set. Review weekly to find the weak stage.
