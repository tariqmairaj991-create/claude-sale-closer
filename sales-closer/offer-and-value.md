# Offers and Value

Selling is far easier when the offer itself is strong. Fix the offer before polishing the pitch.

## 1. What an offer is

An offer is everything the buyer gets, for a price, under certain terms. It includes the result, how it's delivered, support, speed, risk terms and extras. Two products can be identical and the offers very different.

## 2. The four value levers

Buyers judge an offer on four things:

1. **Size of the result:** how much better is their life or business?
2. **Believability:** how sure are they they'll get it?
3. **Speed:** how soon do they see progress?
4. **Effort and hassle:** how much work, risk or inconvenience is required from them?

Raise 1 and 2. Lower 3 and 4. Everything in offer design is a move on one of these.

### Ways to raise the size of the result
- Aim at a bigger, clearer outcome (not "better marketing", but "10 new clients a month").
- Add outcomes they didn't ask for but value (time back, status, peace of mind).
- Target a buyer whose problem is more painful or valuable.

### Ways to raise believability
- Case studies with specific numbers
- A clear, named process ("here are the 4 steps")
- Guarantees (see `pricing-and-guarantees.md`)
- Demonstrations, samples, trials
- Credentials and relevant experience

### Ways to increase speed
- Quick-win deliverable in the first 7 days
- Faster onboarding
- Done-for-you pieces of the first phase

### Ways to reduce effort
- Do more for them (templates, setup, handling the admin)
- Remove steps from the buying process (simple contract, easy payment)
- Provide support so they never feel stuck

## 4. Building an offer: step by step

1. **Pick a specific buyer.** "Dentists with 2-5 locations", not "businesses".
2. **Name their biggest problem or desire** in their words.
3. **List the obstacles** between them and the result.
4. **Turn each obstacle into a deliverable** (what solves it).
5. **Name and package** it clearly.
6. **Add bonuses** that remove common fears or speed results.
7. **Set price** (see pricing file).
8. **Add risk reversal.**
9. **Write the one-sentence promise.**

## 5. The one-sentence promise

Template: "I help [specific buyer] get [specific result] in [timeframe] without [biggest pain or objection]."

Example: "I help local restaurants fill empty weekday tables in 60 days without paying for expensive ads."

Test it: can a stranger repeat it back and say whether it's for them?

## 6. Bonuses that work

Good bonuses reduce a specific fear, speed up a result or make the main thing easier to use. Bad bonuses are random extras that add clutter.

| Buyer fear | Bonus idea |
|---|---|
| "I won't use it" | Onboarding call, done-for-you setup |
| "It'll take too long" | Quick-start checklist, fast-action deliverable |
| "I'll get stuck" | Support channel, monthly check-in |
| "Will it work for me?" | Case studies from similar buyers |

Give every bonus a name, a purpose and a clear value.

## 7. Scarcity and urgency (honest only)

Real reasons to act now: limited capacity, a seasonal window, price changes you've actually planned, bonuses that genuinely end. State the reason and the date. If you wouldn't be comfortable showing the buyer the calendar, it isn't real.

## 8. Diagnosing a weak offer

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Interest, no purchase | Low believability | Add proof, guarantee, demo |
| "Too expensive" often | Result too vague or small | Clarify and quantify the outcome |
| "I'll think about it" | Unclear next step or hidden doubt | Smaller first step, risk reversal |
| No interest at all | Wrong buyer or weak problem | Re-pick audience, sharpen the promise |
| Buy, then churn or refund | Delivery gap or overpromise | Fix onboarding, set honest expectations |

## 9. Stacking and time to value

- **Stacking:** list every related problem standing between the buyer and their goal, create a solution for each, and present them as one complete package. Details and honesty rules are in `growth-and-market.md` (section 12).
- **Time to value:** design a visible first win within days. Buyers purchase for the long-term result but stay because of early progress.

## 10. Quick offer checklist

- [ ] Specific buyer
- [ ] Specific, measurable result
- [ ] Clear timeframe
- [ ] Defined process or deliverables
- [ ] Proof
- [ ] Risk reversal
- [ ] Honest reason to act now
- [ ] One-sentence promise
