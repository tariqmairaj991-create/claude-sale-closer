# Objection Handling

An objection is information. It shows what the buyer still needs to feel certain. Never fight it.

## 1. The four-step method

1. **Listen** to the full objection without interrupting.
2. **Clarify** to find the real concern: "Is that the only thing holding you back?"
3. **Respond** to the real concern with a fact, story, option or question.
4. **Check** that it's resolved: "Does that answer it?" Then return to the decision.

## 2. Clarifying phrases

- "Help me understand, what specifically worries you?"
- "Apart from that, is there anything else?"
- "If we solved that, would you be ready to go ahead?"
- "Is it more about [A] or [B]?"

If the buyer says "if that were solved, yes", you've found the real objection.

## 3. What most objections really are

| Said | Often means |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Value isn't clear, or they can't afford it now |
| "Need to think" | Unspoken doubt |
| "Talk to my partner" | Real need, or politeness |
| "No time" | Not a priority yet |
| "Not sure it works for me" | Doubt in the result or themselves |
| "Send info" | Brush-off or low interest |
| "Using someone else" | Switching risk |

## 4. Playbook

### "It's too expensive."
- Clarify: "Compared to what? What did you expect?"
- If value gap: restate the cost of the problem. "This is costing you about $X a month. If it fixed that, how does the price look?"
- If budget gap: offer a smaller first phase, a deposit or a plan.
- Avoid: instant discounts.

### "I need to think about it."
- "Of course. What part do you want to think over?"
- Address that part honestly.
- Book the follow-up before ending.

### "I need to talk to my partner / boss / team."
- "Makes sense. What do you think they'll ask?"
- Offer: "Would it help if I joined a short call to answer their questions?"
- Provide a one-page summary to share.

### "I don't have the time."
- "What would make this a priority?"
- Show how little time the offer needs, or how much it saves.
- Offer a later start with a deposit.

### "I'm not sure it'll work for me."
- "What worries you most about whether it'd work?"
- Share a similar case.
- Explain the process and support.
- Offer a guarantee or pilot.

### "I've tried something similar and it failed."
- "What happened? What went wrong?"
- Acknowledge, then show specifically how this differs.

### "I'm already working with someone."
- "How's it going? What would you change?"
- Never attack the competitor.
- Offer a small trial or second opinion.

### "Send me some information."
- "Happy to. What would you want to see to make a decision?"
- Send exactly that. Book a follow-up before hanging up.

### "I can't afford it right now."
- "Is it that the budget isn't there, or that you're unsure about the return?"
- If budget: smaller scope, deposit, payment plan, later start.
- If return: revisit the numbers.

### "Can you do it cheaper?"
- "What number did you have in mind?"
- Trade scope for price, not price for nothing: "I can't reduce the fee, but I can remove X and bring it to $Y."

### "I want to compare other options."
- "Great idea. What will you be comparing?"
- Offer the criteria that matter: "Make sure you compare [result, support, track record]."
- Set a date to reconvene.

### "I don't trust it / sounds too good to be true."
- Agree that caution is smart.
- Provide proof: references, case numbers, guarantee, trial.
- Be transparent about limits and what you can't promise.

### "Let me wait until [next month / after the holidays]."
- "What changes then?" If a real reason, set a date.
- If it's avoidance, show the cost of waiting.

### "I need to see more results first."
- Offer a smaller pilot.
- Share the measurable milestones.

## 5. Responding to "no" gracefully

If the buyer has clearly and informedly said no, stop. Thank them, ask one learning question and leave the door open.

> "Totally understand. Thanks for your time. If anything changes, I'm happy to talk again."

## 6. Handling stalls (no reply after the offer)

1. Message within 24h: "Any questions after our call?"
2. Day 3: add value.
3. Day 7: ask directly: "Is this still something you want to solve, or should I close your file?"

## 7. Preventing objections

Most objections can be prevented earlier in the call:
- Qualify budget and decision process in discovery.
- Quantify the problem before price.
- Mention the guarantee in the offer.
- Bring decision-makers onto the call.

## 8. Never

- Invent urgency or fake discounts.
- Argue, interrupt or make the buyer feel stupid.
- Keep pushing after an informed no.
- Answer an objection you haven't clarified.
