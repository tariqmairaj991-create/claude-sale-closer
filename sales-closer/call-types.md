# Call Types and Contexts

Adjust the approach to the sale. Ask the user which situation applies.

## 1. High-ticket service (coaching, agencies, consulting, web design)

- **Cycle:** 1-3 calls. Often a discovery call, then a proposal call.
- **Focus:** diagnose deeply, quantify the problem, build trust.
- **Key moves:**
  - Spend 50-60% of the call in discovery.
  - Present a tailored solution, not a menu.
  - Include a guarantee or pilot to reduce risk.
  - Involve all decision-makers.
- **Watch for:** vague budgets, "send a proposal" brush-offs, scope creep.

## 2. Local business (restaurants, salons, gyms, clinics)

- **Cycle:** same-day to a few days, often in person or by phone.
- **Focus:** quick credibility, simple offer, visible results.
- **Key moves:**
  - Lead with a local example ("I helped [nearby business]").
  - Offer a small, low-risk starting point.
  - Use plain language, no jargon.
  - Close on the spot or book a specific follow-up.

## 3. B2B / company sales

- **Cycle:** weeks to months, several stakeholders.
- **Focus:** map the buyer group, build a business case.
- **Key moves:**
  - Identify the champion, decision-maker, budget holder and blocker.
  - Quantify ROI in their terms.
  - Provide material the champion can forward.
  - Agree on next steps and dates after every meeting.
- **Watch for:** single-threaded deals (only one contact), stalled legal or procurement.

## 4. Low-ticket / e-commerce / digital products

- **Cycle:** minutes. Often no live call.
- **Focus:** clear promise, social proof, easy purchase.
- **Key moves:**
  - Sales page that leads with the result.
  - Strong guarantee.
  - Reviews and specific outcomes.
  - Remove friction from checkout.
  - Follow up abandoned carts honestly.

## 5. Inbound leads (they came to you)

- Respond fast. Speed beats polish.
- Start with "What made you reach out?"
- Qualify early to avoid wasting time.
- Don't assume they're ready to buy; they may be comparing.

## 6. Outbound / cold outreach

- Expect low reply rates; volume and consistent follow-up matter.
- Personalize the first line.
- Lead with relevance, not a pitch.
- Aim for the conversation, not the sale, in the first message.

## 7. Phone vs video vs in person vs chat

| Channel | Tips |
|---|---|
| **Phone** | Smile, slow your pace, take notes, confirm by message after |
| **Video** | Camera on, short slides at most, share screen only for demos |
| **In person** | Dress for the context, bring a one-page summary, mirror their pace |
| **Chat/DM** | Short messages, one question at a time, move to a call when they show interest |

## 8. Selling to different buyer styles

| Style | Cues | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct / results-driven** | Short answers, wants the bottom line | Get to the point, lead with numbers |
| **Analytical** | Asks many detail questions | Provide data, process, proof; give time |
| **Friendly / relationship** | Chatty, values trust | Spend time on connection, share stories |
| **Cautious** | Hesitant, worried about risk | Guarantees, pilots, references, no pressure |

Don't stereotype, but adapt your pace and detail to the person.

## 9. Selling to groups

- Address each person's interest.
- Ask the quietest person a question.
- Find out who has final say early.
- Recap agreements out loud before closing.

## 10. Upselling and renewals

- Wait until the buyer has had a real win.
- Tie the next offer to the next goal they've mentioned.
- Ask: "What's the next thing you want to improve?"
- Don't push something that doesn't fit their goals.
