# Pricing and Guarantees

## 1. Principles

- **Price to the value delivered, not to your time or costs.** A result worth $50,000 to the buyer is not a $500 service.
- **Underpricing hurts.** It signals low quality, attracts difficult buyers and leaves no money for great delivery.
- **Don't discount to win; add value or adjust scope.** Discounting teaches buyers to haggle.
- **Price is a conversation about return.** Always tie it back to what the problem is costing them.

## 2. How to set a price

1. **Estimate the buyer's value:** what is the result worth per month or year?
2. **Check alternatives:** what do competitors, DIY or doing nothing cost?
3. **Check your costs and capacity:** you need healthy margin and time to deliver well.
4. **Start at roughly 10-20% of the value created** as a reference point, then adjust.
5. **Test.** If nobody objects to price, it is probably too low. If most do, test the value story before cutting price.

## 3. Pricing structures

| Structure | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One-off project | Clear deliverables | Scope tightly to avoid creep |
| Monthly retainer | Ongoing work | Stable revenue, define what's included |
| Tiered packages (3 levels) | Most service businesses | Buyers pick the middle; anchor with a premium tier |
| Performance-linked | When results are measurable | Use carefully; protect yourself with clear terms |
| Payment plans | Higher tickets | Prefer a deposit, keep the plan short |

## 4. Three-tier packaging

- **Good:** core result, minimum support.
- **Better (target):** core result, best support, key bonuses.
- **Best:** premium access, speed, done-for-you extras.

The top tier makes the middle look reasonable. Design the middle one to be the real winner.

## 5. Presenting price

1. Restate the value in their words.
2. Say the number clearly, once.
3. Stop talking.

Example: "Everything we discussed (the new booking system, setup and 3 months of support) is $3,500. Does that feel doable?"

Avoid: apologizing ("it's a bit pricey, but..."), rushing, or listing a discount before they object.

## 6. When price is the real issue

If they genuinely can't afford it:
- Offer a smaller scope or first phase.
- Offer a deposit and payment plan.
- Offer a lighter version at a lower price (not the same thing discounted).
- If none works, a respectful no and a future follow-up date.

## 7. Guarantees (risk reversal)

A guarantee takes the risk off the buyer and puts confidence on you. Only offer what you can and will honor.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Money-back** | "If you're not satisfied in 30 days, full refund." |
| **Conditional** | "If you do the steps and don't get X, we keep working free until you do." |
| **Performance** | "If we don't deliver X leads in 60 days, you don't pay for month 3." |
| **Service-based** | "We fix every issue at no cost for 90 days." |
| **Trial or pilot** | "Start with a 2-week paid pilot, then decide." |

Tips:
- Make it specific, measurable and time-bound.
- Make conditions fair and visible (hiding conditions is dishonest).
- Track refund rates. Frequent claims mean the offer or onboarding needs work.

## 8. Anchoring honestly

Anchoring means comparing the price to a reference. Honest anchors: the cost of the problem, the cost of the alternative, the value of the result. Dishonest anchors: fake "original prices" or made-up valuations. Use only the first kind.

## 9. Raising prices

- Raise when you're booked, close above ~50%, or demand exceeds capacity.
- Raise for new customers first; give existing customers notice.
- Pair increases with improved delivery.
