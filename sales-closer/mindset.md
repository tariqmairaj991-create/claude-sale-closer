# Mindset and Ethics

Skill is the smaller half of selling. Most lost deals trace back to how the seller thinks.

## 1. What selling actually is

You are not convincing. You are guiding a decision. The buyer has a problem and a cost of leaving it unsolved. Your job is to make both visible, show a credible path, and ask for a decision.

Test: if the buyer understood everything you know, would they still buy? If yes, sell hard. If no, don't sell.

## 2. The three certainties

Every buyer must be sure of three things before saying yes:

1. **Certainty in the product:** "This works and does what it claims."
2. **Certainty in the seller:** "This person is competent and honest."
3. **Certainty in themselves:** "I can use this and get the result."

When a buyer hesitates, ask which one is missing. Product doubt needs proof (cases, demos, process). Seller doubt needs credibility (listening, honesty, track record). Self doubt needs support (onboarding, guarantee, smaller first step).

## 3. Need, urgency, trust, money

A deal needs four things. Missing one kills it.

| Element | Question to test it |
|---|---|
| **Need** | Is there a real problem or goal? |
| **Urgency** | Why now, not next year? (Find a genuine reason, never invent one.) |
| **Trust** | Do they believe you and the offer? |
| **Money** | Can they pay, or find the money, for the value? |

Most "price objections" are actually trust or urgency problems.

## 4. Handling nervousness and rejection

- Rejection is information about the fit, not about your worth.
- Think in numbers: if you close 1 in 5, every no moves you closer to a yes.
- After every call write one line: what I did well, what to change. Don't replay the failure.
- Nerves fade with reps. Do more calls, not more preparation.

## 5. Language habits that sell

- Say "invest" less and "get" more: speak about what they will receive.
- Replace "I think" and "maybe" with "here's what I'd recommend".
- Use their words, not jargon.
- Talk slower on price and next steps.
- Prefer questions to statements. Questions create ownership.

## 6. Ethics: the line

Acceptable: real deadlines, real capacity limits, honest comparisons, strong belief in your offer, direct asks.

Not acceptable:
- Fake scarcity ("only 2 spots") when untrue
- Invented discounts or inflated "original" prices
- Misleading results or testimonials
- Pressuring someone who clearly can't afford it
- Hiding terms, fees, or cancellation rules
- Exploiting fear or insecurity

Trust compounds: honest sales produce referrals, repeat buyers and fewer refunds. Dishonest ones produce chargebacks and bad reviews.

## 7. The long game

Treat every conversation as a reputation deposit. A respectful "no" today can be a "yes" in six months, or a referral next week.
