# Discovery

Discovery decides the sale. By the time you pitch, the buyer should already feel understood and see the gap between where they are and where they want to be.

## 1. Goals of discovery

1. Understand their situation, goal and problem.
2. Find the cost of the problem and the value of the goal.
3. Learn why now, and what has stopped them so far.
4. Identify who decides, budget and timeline.
5. Decide honestly whether you can help.
6. Build trust through listening.

Aim for them speaking about 70% of the time.

## 2. Question sequence

### A. Open (set the tone)
- "What made you decide to talk now?"
- "What would make this call worthwhile for you?"

### B. Current state
- "Walk me through how this works today."
- "What have you tried? What happened?"
- "Who's currently handling it?"

### C. Problem
- "What's the biggest challenge in this right now?"
- "How long has it been going on?"
- "What's it costing you each month (money, time, stress)?"

### D. Desired state
- "If this were solved, what would be different 6 months from now?"
- "What number or result would make you say it worked?"
- "Why is that important to you?"

### E. Gap and stakes
- "What's stopped you from fixing it already?"
- "What happens if nothing changes this year?"
- "What have you lost because of it?"

### F. Decision and fit
- "Who else needs to be involved in a decision like this?"
- "How do you usually decide on something like this?"
- "What range have you set aside to solve it?"
- "What does your timeline look like?"
- "What would make you confident this is right?"

## 3. Follow-up techniques

- **Echo:** repeat their last 2-3 words as a question. ("Losing customers?")
- **Dig:** "Tell me more about that." / "What do you mean by...?"
- **Quantify:** "How many? How often? How much?"
- **Emotion:** "How does that affect you personally?"
- **Pause:** wait 3 seconds after they answer. They often add the real reason.

## 4. Quantify the problem

Always try to attach a number. Example:

> "You said you miss about 10 calls a week. If each is worth $200 on average, that's around $8,000 a month walking out the door. Is that right?"

Numbers turn a "nice to have" into a priority.

## 5. Qualification checklist

- [ ] Real, felt problem
- [ ] Clear goal
- [ ] Cost of inaction is meaningful
- [ ] Decision-maker present (or access to them)
- [ ] Budget exists or can be found
- [ ] Timeline is realistic
- [ ] Your offer genuinely fits

If several are missing, say so kindly: "I don't think I'm the right fit right now. Here's what I'd suggest." This builds trust and saves time.

## 6. Reflect back (the bridge to the pitch)

Before presenting, summarize in their words:

> "So you want [goal]. Right now [problem] is happening, which costs you about [number]. You've tried [past attempts], and the main thing in the way is [obstacle]. Did I get that right?"

Wait for a yes. If they correct you, adjust. A strong "yes, exactly" is permission to move to the offer.

## 7. Common discovery mistakes

- Pitching too early.
- Asking closed questions (yes/no) only.
- Not asking about money or decision process.
- Letting them stay vague ("we need more sales").
- Taking the first answer as the real one.
- Taking no notes of their exact words.

## 8. Short discovery for DMs or texts

1. "What are you working on right now?"
2. "What's your biggest challenge with [topic]?"
3. "What have you tried?"
4. "If I could help with one thing, what would it be?"
5. Offer a call once they've named a real problem.
